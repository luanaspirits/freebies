Before you decode

Five truths about separation nobody told you.

It isn't only no contact.

Separation is any state where you're not happy with the level of connection, where there's emotional distance. You can be texting every day, or even sleeping next to each other, and still be in it.

"Running" is usually a wound, not a verdict on you.

When someone pulls away, it's often a feeling of I can't let her see me like this. That's a self-worth wound they're carrying. It explains the distance. It doesn't mean you have to sit in it.

You don't have to pretend you don't care.

You were probably told to detach. Here's what I learned instead: union isn't created by pretending that you don't want it. You can want it and still stand in your power.

You're not here to wait forever.

A true connection heals at a reasonable pace and chooses union too. You get to be available for union, and nothing in between: no hot and cold, no breadcrumbs.

Other people's timelines aren't yours.

Reading endless "years of separation" stories can be really inflammatory for your nervous system. It takes time for the physical world to catch up, but you're never stuck in an energy.

Why I made this

My husband and I were in separation for one year and eight months. It wasn't fully no contact. It was distance, plus all the emotional blocks we each had to work through.

There wasn't one magic moment that ended it. It was a series of choices. Part of me wasn't fully choosing union yet, and I had a habit of emotionally withdrawing. When I finally stated clearly what I wanted, and meant it, everything moved.

I didn't force myself to detach. I chose myself, held my standard, and let the connection meet me there. That's what I want for you.
